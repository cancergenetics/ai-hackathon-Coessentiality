## What each paper contributed (for crediting ideas)

- **tsherniak2017**: Introduced the DepMap / Cancer Dependency Map resource and the genome-scale cell-line fitness-screen data we use as input.
- **dempster2021chronos**: Chronos, the cell-population-dynamics gene-effect model; it is the score in our DepMap release (lowest copy-number and screen-quality bias among compared methods).
- **meyers2017ceres**: CERES, the copy-number-effect correction for CRISPR screens; the predecessor score used by the classic co-essentiality papers.
- **pan2018**: Founding evidence that DepMap co-essentiality networks recover complex structure, sub-complex modules and membership (including new SWI/SNF modules). Counts of lines (342 CRISPR / 501 RNAi) come from a press release, not from the paper text.
- **boyle2018**: Olfactory-receptor-gene PCA to remove global/technical correlation before Pearson; showed removal of a few components greatly increases co-functional pairs (53.9% of CORUM within-complex pairs). Source of the "remove top PCs" normalization.
- **kim2019**: Quality filtering, quantile normalization, Pearson network, exclusion of same-chromosome pairs within 20 Mb (copy-number/proximity artefacts), and the note that ETC/mito-ribosome genes dominate.
- **rauscher2018**: Showed gene-specific sgRNA-library batch effects when integrating screens (MINGLE); motivates per-library handling and caution when mixing libraries.
- **wainberg2021**: GLS (whitening the line-by-line covariance after OR-PCA removal) giving calibrated p-values and co-essential modules; our main published baseline ("GLS") and the syntenic-artefact flagging.
- **gheorghe2022**: Factorial benchmark of scores and normalizations; covariance whitening (Cholesky or PCA) is the largest single gain; after whitening Pearson and OLS coincide.
- **hassan2023**: Robust PCA / autoencoder "onion" normalization fusing multiple normalized layers (SNF); supports ensembling across numbers of removed components to recover smaller complexes. AUPRC claims are figure-only.
- **rahman2021flex**: FLEX benchmark; showed two complexes (ETC complex I, mito-ribosome) supply about 76% of true positives and linked the signal to sampling time; motivates per-complex (macro) metrics and saturation at about 300 screens.
- **bacon2025**: BaCoN, a per-gene density/rank balancing of correlations that suppresses hub genes; gains were shown for paralog buffering pairs, not complexes.
- **fischer2025humap**: hu.MAP 3.0 complex atlas; source of our labels and of the complex-level 50:50 train/test split design (Methods text only was read).
- **drew2021humap2**: hu.MAP 2.0; earlier version of the proteomics-integrated complex map; used as reference precision set by Gheorghe and Hart.
- **park2012**: C1/C2/C3 pair-class framework showing random pair CV inflates performance because of per-protein representation; basis of our protein-disjoint evaluation.
- **horvitz1952**: Horvitz-Thompson inverse-probability weighting estimator, credited for weighted estimation under unequal inclusion probabilities.
- **blondel2008**: Louvain community detection for module/cluster extraction.
- **belkin2003**: Laplacian eigenmaps; spectral embedding of a similarity graph.
- **jarvis1973**: Shared-nearest-neighbour similarity for clustering; basis of SNN-type graph features.
- **zhang2005wgcna**: WGCNA framework (soft-threshold adjacency, network construction); related coexpression-network methodology.
- **ravasz2002**: Topological overlap measure and hierarchical modularity; basis of TOM-type neighbourhood-sharing features.
- **ledoit2004**: Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage covariance estimator; relevant to regularizing the line-by-line covariance used in whitening.
- **obayashi2009**: Mutual rank (geometric mean of reciprocal ranks) as a better and more robust coexpression measure than raw correlation; basis of our mutual-rank feature. No DepMap-specific benchmark found.
- **lazar2024**: CRISPR proximity bias from chromosome-arm truncations; same-arm correlations are partly artefactual; justifies discounting same-arm/same-chromosome pairs.
- **saito2015**: Precision-recall is more informative than ROC on imbalanced data; justifies AUPRC as the primary metric.
- **mcdermott2024**: AUPRC preferentially rewards improvements in sub-populations with more positives; justifies pairing pooled AUPRC with macro per-component AUPRC and AUROC.
- **complexportal2022**: Complex Portal, the curated complex source underlying the hu.MAP 3.0 gold standard.
- **hgnc2023**: HGNC gene nomenclature and metadata (symbols, chromosomal location) used for ID mapping and chromosome features.
- **coifman2006**: Diffusion maps; spectral embedding by Markov-chain diffusion, a relative of Laplacian eigenmaps.
- **kish1965**: Source of the Kish effective sample size / design effect for unequal weights, n_eff = (sum w)^2 / sum w^2; section not confirmed (see table).
- **harris2020numpy / virtanen2020scipy / pedregosa2011sklearn / hagberg2008networkx / ke2017lightgbm / paszke2019pytorch / mckinney2010pandas**: Software citations for the numerical and ML stack (arrays, linear algebra/statistics, classical ML, graphs, gradient boosting, neural networks, data frames).
- **lehoucq1998arpack**: ARPACK implicitly restarted Arnoldi eigensolver (behind scipy sparse eigsh), credited for the sparse spectral embeddings.
- **williams2001nystrom**: Nyström low-rank kernel/eigen approximation, credited for the landmark-based spectral approximation.
- **field2007cluster**: Cluster (block) bootstrap theory for clustered data; credited for resampling complexes/clusters rather than pairs.
- **depmap26q3**: Data release citation (DepMap asks to cite the figshare record of the release used); DOI UNVERIFIED.
- **humap3web**: hu.MAP 3.0 web portal, accessed 2026-10-27.

## Discrepancies and notes on LITERATURE.md

1. Wainberg 2021: LITERATURE.md writes "…, Kundaje A, Bassik MC"; the last two authors are in the order Bassik MC, then Kundaje A (last author). The citation here uses the first six authors only, so the manuscript is unaffected.
2. Obayashi and Kinoshita: LITERATURE.md gives "DNA Res (2009)" with no volume/pages; verified as DNA Res. 2009;16(5):249–60, doi:10.1093/dnares/dsp016. The key in the task brief says "obayashi2008"; the year is 2009 (first online 18 Sep 2009). An incorrect DOI guess (dsn032) resolves to an unrelated paper; use dsp016.
3. Lazar 2024: LITERATURE.md gives no authors beyond "Lazar NH, …, (Recursion)"; the paper has 11 authors (first six above). Volume/pages 56:1482–1493 confirmed (issue 7, July 2024).
4. McDermott 2024: LITERATURE.md has "McDermott M, et al."; the full first author is Matthew B. A. McDermott (McDermott MBA). NeurIPS 2024 venue confirmed on arXiv 2401.06091 (v4). Proceedings volume number (37) is the NeurIPS 2024 volume by convention; the sources found do not list volume or pages.
5. Fischer 2025 (hu.MAP 3.0): LITERATURE.md pages 911–943 are confirmed by PubMed (21(7):911–43). Crossref instead lists article number 8 for the same item; PubMed pagination is used.
6. Hassan 2023: Mol Syst Biol 19(11):e11657 matches LITERATURE.md; online publication Sep 2023 (Crossref) / Nov 2023 issue (PubMed).
7. LITERATURE.md bibliographic details for Pan 2018, Boyle 2018, Kim 2019, Rauscher 2018, Gheorghe 2022, Rahman 2021, Rohde 2025, Park 2012, Dempster 2021 all match PubMed/Crossref. Their content claims were not re-verified here (see the [verified]/[abstract-only] tags in LITERATURE.md).
8. Pan 2018: the 342 CRISPR / 501 RNAi line counts are cited in LITERATURE.md from a press release and were not checked against the paper; avoid quoting them in the manuscript.
9. Mol Syst Biol, Life Sci Alliance and PLoS One use article numbers (eNNNN) instead of page ranges; Vancouver strings above use them accordingly. Genome Biol and BMC Bioinformatics use article numbers without "e".
10. Kish 1965 and the exact location of the effective-sample-size formula were not verified against the book text; if the manuscript cites a specific section or page, check it in the original.
